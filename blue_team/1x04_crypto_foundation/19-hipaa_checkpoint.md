# The HIPAA Crypto Checkpoint

**Important framing note**: HIPAA's "addressable" implementation specifications are not optional — a covered entity must either implement the specified safeguard, or implement a documented equivalent alternative and record the reasoning. "We didn't get to it" is not a valid alternative under either path. Two of the four requirements below are addressable in this sense; two (transmission security's parent standard, and authentication) are not optional at all.

## HIPAA Crypto Compliance Table

| Requirement | Citation | Current MedDefense State | Compliant? | Gap / Remediation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Encryption/decryption of ePHI** (at rest) | §164.312(a)(2)(iv) — addressable | Per Task 0's Data Protection Map: PostgreSQL (ehr-db-01), MySQL (billing-srv-01), and DICOM storage (pacs-srv-01) all have **zero** encryption at rest. No documented equivalent alternative exists either. | **No** | Implement AES-256 TDE for the EHR and billing databases, and encrypted storage for DICOM images (Task 13/15's recommendations) — with no compensating control currently documented, there's no addressable-alternative defense available. |
| **Transmission security** (parent standard) | §164.312(e)(1) — required standard, not addressable itself | Mixed: the patient portal has weak TLS (1x02 Finding 005), database connections permit unencrypted sessions (`hostnossl` alongside `hostssl`), and DICOM traffic is fully cleartext (1x02 Finding 024). | **No** — a required standard needs a genuinely reasonable safeguard across the board, and multiple major ePHI flows have none at all | Comprehensive review and remediation across every ePHI transmission path: enforce `hostssl`-only DB connections, enable DICOM TLS, remove TLS 1.0 from the portal. |
| **Encryption of ePHI in transit** | §164.312(e)(2)(ii) — addressable | DICOM traffic: **none** (Finding 024). EHR DB connections: **partial/weak** (SSL available but not enforced). Portal: **weak** (TLS 1.0 still permitted, Finding 005). | **No** | Enforce TLS 1.2+ everywhere ePHI moves — no exceptions for DICOM, the DB layer, or the portal (Tasks 11 and 13/15 cover the specific configuration). |
| **Person or entity authentication** | §164.312(d) — **required**, not addressable | MFA exists on exactly one personal account in the entire organization (1x03 findings). Active Directory still permits RC4/DES Kerberos encryption and unsigned LDAP (1x02 Findings 018, 007). | **No** | Deploy MFA broadly for any ePHI-adjacent access (not just one executive account), disable RC4/DES as Kerberos encryption types, and require LDAP signing — all already scoped in the 1x03 remediation roadmap. |

## Could MedDefense Pass a HIPAA Security Audit Today?

No — not on the encryption requirements specifically, and not by a wide margin. Every one of the four requirements checked above comes back non-compliant, including **Person or Entity Authentication**, which isn't an addressable specification MedDefense could argue an equivalent alternative for — it's a required standard, and the current state (MFA on one account, weak Kerberos encryption still enabled) doesn't meet it under any reasonable reading. The single most critical deficiency an auditor would almost certainly cite first is the **complete absence of encryption at rest on ehr-db-01, combined with that database being directly reachable from the entire internal network with no access restriction at all** (1x02 Finding 003) — this is the starkest possible example of "no safeguard and no documented equivalent alternative" on the single most obviously regulated dataset MedDefense holds, and it's the finding that would be hardest to explain away, since an addressable-specification defense requires showing *some* compensating measure was in place, and none exists here at all.
